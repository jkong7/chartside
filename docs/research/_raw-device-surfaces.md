# Device and OS Surfaces for a "Ghost" Scribe Front Door (raw research, 2026-09-28)

Scope: where a no-app or near-no-app front door for an ambient AI scribe could live in late 2026, weighted toward what an ordinary person (and an ordinary clinician) already touches daily. The core technical question on every surface is the same: can it reliably capture 15 to 60 minutes of audio, including with the screen locked or the app backgrounded, and get it to our server under a BAA?

Context on versions: iOS 26 shipped September 15, 2025, and iOS 27 shipped September 14, 2026 ([Wikipedia iOS 26](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IOS_26), [AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/26/09/09/ios-27-arrives-on-september-14-heres-what-youll-get)). iOS 27's headline is a rebuilt "Siri AI" with its own app, on-screen awareness, and more in-app actions on iPhone 15 Pro and newer, plus shortcut creation by describing it in natural language ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/09/13/ios-27-release-date-new-features/)). Most of the surfaces below were introduced in iOS 18 and iOS 26 and carry forward.

## 1. iOS surfaces

### 1.1 Action button, Control Center, Lock Screen controls

iOS 18 opened Control Center to third-party "controls" built with the WidgetKit Controls API (`ControlWidget`). The same control can be placed in Control Center, can replace the flashlight and camera buttons at the bottom of the Lock Screen, and can be mapped to the Action button on iPhone 15 Pro and newer ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2024/06/10/developers-toggles-control-center-ios-18/), [MacRumors Control Center guide](https://www.macrumors.com/guide/ios-18-control-center/), [Apple ControlWidget docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/controlwidget)). A control can toggle state, run an App Intent, or deep link.

What this means for a scribe: "press the Action button, the visit starts recording" is a real, shippable gesture, and the Lock Screen button is a one-tap start that doctors would use without unlocking. But every one of these requires a native app with a widget extension. There is no web-app path to a control. The closest web-only approximation is a Shortcut the user builds (see 1.3), which the Action button can also run.

### 1.2 Live Activities and Dynamic Island

Live Activities (ActivityKit plus WidgetKit) render an ongoing task on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island; the system handles compact, minimal, and expanded layouts ([Newly guide](https://newly.app/guides/dynamic-island), [Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-kw/guide/iphone/iph28f50d10d/ios)). For a scribe this is the ideal "ghost" status surface: a running timer, a pause button, and a "note ready" state on the Lock Screen with no app on screen. The OS also shows its own orange microphone indicator whenever any app is recording.

Background recording on native iOS is well defined: with `UIBackgroundModes` including `audio`, an app that is actively recording keeps running in the background and when the screen locks; when recording stops, the system suspends it ([Apple UIBackgroundModes](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/information-property-list/uibackgroundmodes), summarized in [GetStream background modes](https://getstream.io/video/docs/ios/advanced/background-modes/)). Developers report that updating a Live Activity from a backgrounded audio app is flaky on real devices even when it works in the simulator, so push-driven Live Activity updates from the server are the safer design ([Apple forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/tags/avaudiosession?page=2)).

### 1.3 App Intents, Shortcuts, "Get Contents of URL", Apple Intelligence

App Intents is now Apple's main API into an app from Siri, Spotlight, Shortcuts, widgets, controls, and Apple Intelligence ([Blake Crosley](https://blakecrosley.com/blog/app-intents-are-apples-new-api-to-your-app)). iOS 26 added interactive snippets (small SwiftUI cards with buttons shown inside Siri, Spotlight, or Visual Intelligence without opening the app), Visual Intelligence image search, on-screen entities, undoable intents, multiple-choice prompts, and explicit "supported modes" so an intent can declare `.background` or foreground continuation ([WWDC25 session 275](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/275/), [Superwall](https://superwall.com/blog/app-intents-interactive-snippets-in-ios-26)). The Shortcuts app in iOS 26 gained a "Use Model" action that calls Apple Intelligence models (on-device, Private Cloud Compute, or ChatGPT), and WWDC26 added shortcut storage and a model transcript inspector ([WWDC26 session 310](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/310/)).

The no-install angle: a Shortcut distributed as an iCloud link can chain built-in actions such as "Record Audio" (opens a recording sheet), then "Get Contents of URL" with a POST of the file to our HTTPS endpoint, then "Open URL" to the note. That runs from the Action button, Back Tap, Siri, or a Home Screen icon, and needs zero code from Apple's side. The weaknesses: the Record Audio sheet is foreground and interactive, it is not designed for hour-long capture, "Get Contents of URL" uploads happen after the recording rather than streaming, and adding a third-party Shortcut link requires the user to accept a trust prompt. It is a strong demo and power-user path, not a reliable default. Note that Siri AI in iOS 27 can only act inside apps that expose App Intents, which again means native code ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/09/13/ios-27-release-date-new-features/)).

### 1.4 Voice Memos, Notes, and Phone call recording

Since iOS 18, Voice Memos and Notes transcribe recordings on-device for free (iPhone 12 or newer, no Apple Intelligence required), and iOS 18.1 added call recording and transcription in the Phone app where legally supported ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/how-to/ios-record-audio-transcribe-notes-app/), [AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/inside/ios-18/tips/how-to-record-audio-and-create-transcripts-in-notes-in-ios-18), [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/phones/iphones/ios-18-finally-adds-transcription-to-voice-memos-heres-how-to-use-it)). Call recordings play an audible announcement to all parties and land in Notes with a transcript; with Apple Intelligence they also get a summary. Voice Memos transcription covers roughly ten languages; Notes transcription is English-only variants ([Kiolo](https://kiolo.com/en/blog/apple-voice-memo-transcription/), [Popular Science](https://www.popsci.com/diy/how-to-record-audio-make-transcripts-iphone/)).

This is the single most important finding for a "ghost" front door: every iPhone owner already has a free, reliable, lock-screen-safe hour-long recorder with a transcript. Voice Memos records for hours with the screen off. The integration seam is the Share sheet: a user shares the memo (audio file, or the transcript text) out. A native share extension would put "ChartSide" in that sheet. Without native code, the options are (a) a Shortcut that accepts shared audio and POSTs it, (b) "Save to Files" into a synced folder we ingest, or (c) email or text the file to a unique address or number. Apple's on-device transcript is not a clinical-grade transcript, so we would re-transcribe the audio, but the capture problem is solved for free.

### 1.5 Web apps on iOS: what Safari 26 can and cannot do

iOS 26 made every site added to the Home Screen open as a standalone web app by default, even with no manifest, with an "Open as Web App" toggle ([WebKit Safari 26.0](https://webkit.org/blog/17333/webkit-features-in-safari-26-0/), [MacRumors how-to](https://www.macrumors.com/how-to/save-safari-bookmark-web-app-iphone-home-screen/), [Michael Tsai](https://mjtsai.com/blog/2025/10/03/web-apps-in-ios-26/)). Web Push has worked for Home Screen web apps since iOS 16.4; Safari 18.4 added Declarative Web Push (no service worker required) and Screen Wake Lock in Home Screen web apps ([WebKit Safari 18.4](https://webkit.org/blog/16574/webkit-features-in-safari-18-4/), [Meet Declarative Web Push](https://webkit.org/blog/16535/meet-declarative-web-push/)). Safari 26 added ALAC and PCM in MediaRecorder and WebCodecs AudioEncoder, which helps lossless or raw capture ([WebKit Safari 26.0](https://webkit.org/blog/17333/webkit-features-in-safari-26-0/)). Media Session is supported (lock-screen metadata and controls for playback).

The hard wall is background capture. In Safari and Home Screen web apps, WebRTC and Web Audio are suspended when the screen locks or the page goes to the background, and `getUserMedia` microphone tracks are muted ([Apple forums 774239](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/774239), [MagicBell](https://www.magicbell.com/blog/pwa-ios-limitations-safari-support-complete-guide), [Apple forums 762582](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/762582)). A February 2025 request for a background WebRTC permission has no Apple reply. The WebKit bug for background mic muting was closed "configuration changed" with the fix being for WKWebView apps to declare the `audio` background mode, which a Safari web app cannot do; a 2024 comment says Home Screen web apps still lose the mic ([WebKit bug 226620](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=226620)).

Practical implication: an iOS web recorder works for a 15 to 60 minute visit only if the screen stays on and the page stays foreground. Screen Wake Lock (18.4+) keeps the screen from auto-locking, which makes this viable for a phone lying on the desk, but a physician pocketing the phone or pressing the side button kills capture. Chunked upload (for example 10 second segments via MediaRecorder `timeslice`) limits loss when that happens. Other caveats: iOS web apps get no Background Sync or Background Fetch, script-writable storage is evicted after about 7 days of non-use, and in the EU under the DMA, Home Screen web apps have been degraded since iOS 17.4 ([MagicBell](https://www.magicbell.com/blog/pwa-ios-limitations-safari-support-complete-guide)).

### 1.6 App Clips

App Clips are small native slices of an app launched without an install from App Clip Codes, NFC tags, QR codes, Safari banners, Messages links, and Maps; iOS 17 raised the size limit to 50 MB for digital-only invocations while physical invocations (QR, NFC, App Clip Code) stay at 15 MB, and iOS 17 added App Clip Suggestions on the Lock Screen ([Bugfender](https://bugfender.com/blog/ios-app-clips/), [Very Good Security](https://www.verygoodsecurity.com/blog/posts/optimizing-appclip-size-in-ios), [WWDC23](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10178)). App Clips support ephemeral notifications (roughly 8 hours after launch) and hand off to the full app via an in-clip App Store overlay.

For a scribe: a QR sticker in the exam room that launches a native recorder with no install is attractive on paper. The catches: App Clips cannot use background execution modes and are restricted from a set of frameworks (Apple's list includes Speech and several privacy-sensitive kits), so reliable locked-screen audio capture is not something to plan on; the App Clip still requires writing and shipping Swift through App Review; and developers regularly hit "App Clip Unavailable" invocation failures with QR codes ([Apple forums 748209](https://origin-devforums.apple.com/forums/thread/748209), [Apple forums 757993](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/757993)). Treat App Clips as a zero-install onboarding and consent surface, not the recorder. (Apple's primary App Clip docs did not render through our fetcher; confirm the exact framework and background-mode restrictions against developer.apple.com before building.)

### 1.7 iMessage apps

iMessage app extensions (Messages framework) are native extensions shipped inside an iOS app. There is nothing new here since 2016 for our purposes, and there is no web equivalent. A lighter pattern that needs no extension: a Messages link to our site renders a rich preview, and iOS 26 web app default behavior makes "tap link, Add to Home Screen" shorter. Messages is better as a delivery channel (send the patient summary or the "note ready" link) than as a capture surface.

## 2. Android 16

**Quick Settings tiles.** `TileService` lets any app add a Quick Settings tile. Android 12+ forbids starting foreground services from the background, and Android 14+ adds that a microphone foreground service needs the while-in-use `RECORD_AUDIO` permission, which a backgrounded app does not hold. Exemptions include starts triggered by app widgets, notifications, a PendingIntent from a visible app, or system components ([Android FGS restrictions](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/restrictions-bg-start)). In practice a tile that launches a trampoline activity and then a `microphone`-type foreground service is the standard pattern and gives a one-swipe start from the shade. Native only.

**Live Updates.** Android 16 adds `Notification.ProgressStyle` for start-to-end journeys shown on the lock screen and shade, which is Android's equivalent of a Live Activity for a "recording, 12:43" status ([Android 16 features](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/16/features)).

**Gemini.** AppFunctions lets an Android 16 app expose `@AppFunction` tools that Gemini and other agents can call, described by Google as an on-device MCP server; as of May 2026 the Gemini integration is private preview with trusted testers only ([Android AppFunctions](https://developer.android.com/ai/appfunctions)). Not a 2026 launch dependency.

**PWA and TWA background recording.** Chrome on Android is materially better than Safari: an installed PWA or a Trusted Web Activity wrapper can keep a `getUserMedia` stream alive with a persistent "recording" media notification while the user switches apps, though aggressive OEM battery managers can still kill long sessions. This is from practitioner experience rather than a spec guarantee, so it needs device testing on Samsung and Pixel. A TWA is a thin Android app wrapping the PWA, publishable to Play, which also unlocks a native Quick Settings tile if we add a small amount of Kotlin.

**Web Share Target.** An installed PWA can register as a share target and receive files (any MIME type declared in the manifest, so `audio/*` works) via Chrome 76+ on Android and Chrome 89+ on desktop; the user must install the PWA first ([Chrome Web Share Target](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/capabilities/web-apis/web-share-target)). This is the Android equivalent of the iOS "share the Voice Memo" flow, and it is fully web. Safari does not support Web Share Target.

## 3. Wearables and ambient hardware

**Meta Ray-Ban and Ray-Ban Display.** The Meta Wearables Device Access Toolkit (DAT) was announced September 18, 2025 as a developer preview for iOS and Android, giving apps camera and audio access on Meta AI glasses; publishing to the general public was slated for 2026, with only select partners publishing during preview ([Meta DAT intro](https://developers.meta.com/blog/introducing-meta-wearables-device-access-toolkit/)). On May 14, 2026 Meta opened Ray-Ban Display to third-party developers with two paths: native SDKs, and plain HTML/CSS/JS web apps deployed by URL via Developer Mode in the Meta AI app, shareable to testers via password-protected URLs or release channels of up to 100 testers ([Meta blog](https://developers.meta.com/blog/build-for-display-glasses/), [Meta FAQ](https://developers.meta.com/wearables/faq/), [gHacks](https://www.ghacks.net/2026/05/18/meta-opens-ray-ban-display-glasses-to-third-party-developers-through-wearables-toolkit/)). The iOS SDK README marks audio streaming as "experimental; unavailable for production publishing" ([GitHub meta-wearables-dat-ios](https://github.com/facebook/meta-wearables-dat-ios)), and the web-app path documentation does not mention microphone access. Social context: New York banned camera and mic glasses in state courts in July 2026 and England and Wales followed in August 2026 ([Wikipedia Ray-Ban Meta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray-Ban_Meta)). Glasses in exam rooms will draw patient-consent objections. Good for a demo video, not a 2026 product surface.

**Apple Watch.** Every Watch has a mic and a Voice Memos app, and watchOS native apps can record audio, but wrist-down background recording is constrained by power and session rules; our fetch of Apple's watchOS audio doc did not return usable content, so treat hour-long Watch capture as unverified. The Watch is more useful as a remote: a complication or Smart Stack widget that starts and stops the phone recorder, and a haptic "note ready".

**Plaud.** Plaud claims 1.5 million users, and sells NotePin S (about $159 to $169) and Note Pro (about $189) with a $17.99/month plan; it markets HIPAA, SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001, and a BAA for healthcare orgs ([Plaud healthcare](https://www.plaud.ai/pages/healthcare-solution), [Plaud NotePin S](https://www.plaud.ai/products/plaud-notepin-s), [Plaud clinical blog](https://sea.plaud.ai/blogs/articles/5-best-ai-note-takers-for-clinical-documentation-in-2026)). There is no native EHR integration; notes are copied and pasted ([iatroX comparison](https://www.iatrox.com/blog/plaud-notepin-vs-heidi-remote-vs-omi-wearable-ai-scribe-devices-clinicians-compared-2026)). Plaud audio exports as files, so the realistic integration is "export audio to ChartSide" via share sheet or email. Plaud is also a direct competitor for solo clinicians.

**Limitless.** Acquired by Meta, announced December 5, 2025. Pendant sales stopped, existing owners get about a year of support, the Rewind Mac app shut down December 19, 2025, and service ended in the EU and UK ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/meta-acquires-ai-device-startup-210213488.html), [Hedy](https://www.hedy.ai/post/meta-acquires-limitless-ai-privacy/), [AI Business](https://aibusiness.com/speech-recognition/meta-acquires-limitless)). Dead as a target.

**Bee.** Amazon agreed to acquire Bee in July 2025; Bee is a $49.99 wristband or clip pendant that records ambient audio and produces summaries and to-dos, and it still ships under the Bee brand ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/22/amazon-acquires-bee-the-ai-wearable-that-records-everything-you-say/), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/07/22/amazon-ai-bee-wearable.html), [Layer3 review](https://www.layer3labs.io/gear/reviews/bee-ai-pendant)). Consumer, closed, no BAA. Not a target.

**Omi.** Open-source hardware, firmware, app, and backend from Based Hardware, around $89, with its own app store and SDK, claiming 300,000+ users ([GitHub BasedHardware/omi](https://github.com/BasedHardware/omi), [Omi docs](https://docs.omi.me/doc/get_started/introduction)). The open plugin model means an "Omi app" that forwards transcripts or audio to ChartSide is buildable in TypeScript against webhooks. HIPAA claims are unverified and there is no clear BAA, so it is a hobbyist channel.

**Clinician-specific.** Heidi Remote is a dedicated device with on-device transcription via Argmax, bundled with Heidi ([iatroX](https://www.iatrox.com/blog/plaud-notepin-vs-heidi-remote-vs-omi-wearable-ai-scribe-devices-clinicians-compared-2026)). The enterprise norm is still the smartphone clipped to the badge or placed on the desk, with Microsoft offering room microphones for DAX/Dragon Copilot ([Alignify](https://alignify.co/blog/medical-scribe), [patientnotes.ai](https://patientnotes.ai/resources/ambient-listening-healthcare)). Market share context: Abridge won Best in KLAS 2025 and 2026 and DAX holds roughly a third of ambient share ([Sully](https://www.sully.ai/blog/best-10-ai-medical-scribes-in-2025)). Takeaway: incumbents did not win on hardware; they won on the phone the doctor already carries.

## 4. Telephony and voice agents

The "call a phone number and put it on speaker" front door is the most layman-familiar zero-install surface that exists: every phone, landline, and flip phone can do it, and a phone call on speaker does not stop when the screen locks. Audio quality is the tradeoff (8 kHz narrowband over PSTN versus 16 to 48 kHz from an app).

**Twilio Media Streams.** Forks raw call audio to your WebSocket. Unidirectional streams can carry inbound, outbound, or both tracks (up to 4 per call); bidirectional (`<Connect><Stream>`) allows one stream per call with the inbound track only, and lets you play audio back ([Twilio docs](https://www.twilio.com/docs/voice/media-streams)). Price is $0.004/min plus voice minutes ([Twilio blog](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/blog/media-streams-public-beta)). US inbound local is $0.0085/min plus $1.15/month per number; recording is $0.0025/min ([Twilio US voice pricing](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/voice/pricing/us)). For a passive scribe line (no bot talking), Media Streams into our own STT is the cheapest option: roughly $0.0125/min Twilio plus about $0.005 to $0.008/min for Deepgram Nova-3 streaming ([Deepgram pricing](https://deepgram.com/pricing)), or under $1 for a 60 minute visit. Twilio signs BAAs for HIPAA-eligible services ([Twilio HIPAA](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/hipaa)).

**Twilio ConversationRelay.** Twilio handles STT, TTS, and turn-taking and sends text to your LLM over WebSocket. $0.07/min plus voice ([Twilio AI pricing](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/products/conversational-ai/pricing)); HIPAA eligible since March 17, 2025 ([Twilio changelog](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/changelog/conversationrelay-is-now-hipaa-eligible)). Useful for an interactive "voice intake" or "dictate your addendum" agent, overkill for passive capture.

**Deepgram Voice Agent API.** Standard $0.075/min, Advanced $0.163/min pay-as-you-go, with cheaper bring-your-own-LLM tiers (reported $0.065/min after an August 2026 increase) ([Deepgram pricing](https://deepgram.com/pricing), [Cekura](https://www.cekura.ai/blogs/deepgram-pricing)). BAA via enterprise agreement.

**OpenAI Realtime over SIP.** Point a SIP trunk (Twilio, Telnyx) at `sip:$PROJECT_ID@sip.api.openai.com;transport=tls`; a webhook fires on inbound calls and you accept via `/v1/realtime/calls/{call_id}/accept`, with refer and hangup verbs. gpt-realtime audio is $32 per 1M input tokens and $64 per 1M output tokens, roughly $0.05 to $0.15 per conversational minute; latency is 500 to 1,200 ms for the first turn and 300 to 600 ms steady ([Forasoft](https://www.forasoft.com/blog/article/openai-realtime-api-webrtc-sip-websockets-integration), [OpenAI Realtime SIP guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/realtime-sip)). HIPAA use requires a signed BAA plus Zero Data Retention. For a 60 minute passive visit, a speech-to-speech model is the wrong tool; use it only for short interactive agent turns.

**Vapi and Retell.** Vapi charges $0.05/min hosting plus pass-through provider costs, with HIPAA as a $2,000/month add-on and zero data retention a separate $1,000/month; Retell prices $0.07 to $0.31/min all-in and includes HIPAA and a self-serve BAA on all plans ([Ringlyn](https://www.ringlyn.com/blog/vapi-vs-retell-ai/), [Retell blog, vendor source](https://www.retellai.com/blog/retell-vs-vapi)). Latency: Retell about 600 ms default; Vapi advertises under 500 ms but its own docs say about 800 ms typical ([Cekura](https://www.cekura.ai/blogs/retell-vs-vapi)). Both are built for agents that talk, not passive recorders, and both add margin on top of the Twilio plus Deepgram floor.

## 5. Browser surfaces

**Chrome side panel.** Extensions can host a persistent side panel (Chrome 114+) that opens on toolbar click and can be global or per-tab ([Chrome sidePanel API](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/reference/api/sidePanel)). For a clinician using a web EHR, a side panel sitting next to the chart is the natural "ghost" surface: record in the panel, paste the note into the EHR tab. Mic capture in MV3 is done via an offscreen document with the `USER_MEDIA` reason, which unlike `AUDIO_PLAYBACK` has no 30 second auto-close ([Chrome offscreen API](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/reference/api/offscreen)). Distribution through the Chrome Web Store, typically one click, but many hospital IT departments block unapproved extensions.

**Chrome built-in AI (Gemini Nano).** Summarizer, Translator, and Language Detector have been stable since Chrome 138; the Prompt API is available to web pages (reported stable in Chrome 148, Q2 2026) and accepts audio input on machines with a GPU. Requirements: desktop only (Windows, macOS 13+, Linux, Chromebook Plus), 22 GB free disk, more than 4 GB VRAM or 16 GB RAM ([Chrome Prompt API](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/ai/prompt-api), [Chrome built-in AI](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/ai/built-in), [Summarizer API](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/ai/summarizer-api)). Useful for on-device PHI redaction or a draft summary before upload, but not clinical-grade and not on phones.

**Arc and Dia.** Arc is in maintenance mode since May 2025; Atlassian bought The Browser Company for $610 million in September 2025 and Dia is the AI-browser focus ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Browser_Company)). Dia is Chromium, so a Chrome extension and web app work there too. Not a distinct surface worth targeting.

**Desktop overlays (Cluely).** Cluely popularized an always-on-top desktop overlay that listens to meeting audio and is hidden from screen share; it raised $20.3 million, rebranded from "cheat on everything" to a meeting assistant by November 2025, and its CEO admitted in 2026 to misstating revenue ([Wikipedia Cluely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cluely)). The UX idea (a tiny floating pill that sees and hears the session) is directly relevant for telehealth visits; the brand association is not. Overlays require Electron or Tauri plus OS-level audio capture permissions.

**Document Picture-in-Picture.** `documentPictureInPicture.requestWindow()` opens an always-on-top window with arbitrary HTML; Chrome and Edge 116+, Firefox 151+, no Safari, desktop only; it requires a user gesture and never outlives the opener tab ([Chrome docs](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/web-platform/document-picture-in-picture/), [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Document_Picture-in-Picture_API)). The recorder pattern (start button, level meter, timer floating over the EHR or Zoom) is documented by screen-recorder vendors ([Contrast](https://www.getcontrast.io/learn/using-document-picture-in-picture-and-insertable-streams-apis-to-record-your-screen-and-camera)). This is the web's answer to Cluely with no install: pure Next.js, a floating recorder that stays visible across windows while the originating tab holds the mic. Desktop Chrome does not suspend mic capture in a background tab, so a 60 minute capture is reliable as long as the tab is not closed.

## 6. Install friction: App Clip vs PWA vs native

Hard numbers are thin, so here is what exists:

- Average App Store product-page conversion (view to install) is about 8.6% in the US; Google Play is about 16.2% ([AppTweak 2025](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/average-app-conversion-rate-per-category)). That is before account creation and mic permission.
- The oft-cited heuristic that every onboarding step costs about 20% of users ([Gabor Cselle](https://blog.gaborcselle.com/2012/10/every-step-costs-you-20-of-users.html)) is directionally useful but disputed even in its own comments.
- Size: Twitter Lite's PWA was 600 KB versus 23.5 MB for the native Android app, and drew 250,000 daily home-screen launches ([web.dev case study](https://web.dev/case-studies/twitter)).
- Step counts, iOS: native app = App Store page, Get, Face ID, wait, open, sign in, grant mic (about 7 steps). Home Screen web app = open link, Share, Add to Home Screen, Add, open, grant mic (about 6 steps, and iOS never shows an automatic install prompt). Plain web page = open link, grant mic (2 steps). App Clip = scan QR or tap NFC, tap Open card, grant mic (3 steps), capped at 15 MB for physical codes.
- Step counts, phone number: dial, put on speaker (2 steps, no permission prompt, no account if we identify by caller ID plus a PIN).

For clinicians specifically, the gating factor is often IT approval rather than taps: a URL and a phone number usually pass, an extension or App Store app often does not.

## 7. Ranked surfaces

Scoring: 5 is best. "Reliability" means 15 to 60 minute capture surviving screen lock, pocketing, and app switching. "Build effort" is for a TypeScript/Next.js team with no native developers (5 = easiest). "Testability" is how easily we can automate and reproduce (Playwright, simulators, real devices).

| Rank | Surface | Layman familiarity | Zero-install | 15-60 min reliability | Build effort (TS team) | Testability | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Plain web page (Next.js) on desktop + Document PiP floating recorder | 4 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 5 | Best for telehealth and desk-based visits; Chrome/Edge/Firefox desktop only for PiP |
| 2 | Phone number on speaker (Twilio Media Streams + Deepgram) | 5 | 5 | 5 | 4 | 4 | Works on any phone with screen locked; 8 kHz audio; about $1/hour cost floor |
| 3 | Mobile web page with Wake Lock, screen-on, chunked upload | 5 | 5 | 2 (iOS) / 4 (Android) | 5 | 4 | Fails on iOS if pocketed or locked; fine if phone sits on desk |
| 4 | Record in Voice Memos, share file to us (Shortcut, email, Files, Web Share Target on Android) | 5 | 4 | 5 | 4 | 3 | Capture is Apple's problem; hand-off step adds friction and delay |
